How I work

80% research. 20% writing. Every word aimed at results.

Most copy gets written the other way round. A short brief, a blank page, a lot of typing.

I spend most of a project before the first draft.

I research your audience until I can describe their day: what they want, what scares them, and the exact words they use when they complain. I read your reviews, comments, support emails, forum threads and sales call notes.

Then I study your competitors. What do they all promise? Where do they go quiet? In that gap is the thing only you can say. That is your selling point, and every line I write leans on it.

That is the strategy. The writing is the last 20%. It moves fast, because by then I know who I'm talking to and what will make them act.

The job isn't finished when the copy sounds good. It's finished when the numbers move: sign-ups, calls, sales, donations.

1. Research the audience.

Reviews, comments, DMs, tickets, forums and sales calls. I collect what people say in their own words until the same wants and fears keep repeating.

2. Study the competition.

I map what every competitor promises, find the gap, and pin down your unique selling point: the reason to choose you that nobody else can claim.

3. Write.

Headline, offer, proof and call to action, built on that research and on the psychology of why people buy. This is the 20%.

4. Measure the result.

Sign-ups, calls, sales, donations. That is what the copy is for, and it's how I judge my own work.

The books I keep going back to

I've read Ogilvy on Advertising, Influence, Scientific Advertising, Great Leads and The Adweek Copywriting Handbook a few times each.

They taught me why people buy. We decide with feeling, then look for reasons to defend the decision. We trust what people like us have already chosen. We hate losing something more than we enjoy gaining it.

That psychology is in every headline, offer and button I write.
